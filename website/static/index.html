    What Are Disposable Email Addresses?

    And why are they a security concern?

    Definition

    Disposable-email services provide shared, masked, or short-lived addresses. A provider-domain match identifies the service category but does not prove that an individual mailbox expires.

    Why They Are Risky

    • Temporary inboxes can reduce sender accountability
    • They may be used to bypass account-verification policies
    • Privacy relays also have legitimate safety and privacy uses
    • A domain match is evidence, not proof of malicious intent

    Known Temporary-Email Examples

    mailinator.com guerrillamail.com 10minutemail.com tempmail.com yopmail.com maildrop.cc trashmail.com getnada.com

    How do I get the original .eml email?

    An original .eml keeps the sender’s real address, the real link targets and your mail service’s SPF/DKIM/DMARC results. Screenshots and copied text lose them.

    • Gmail (web): open the email → ⋮ (More) → Download message. After uploading, choose Gmail under “Downloaded from”.
    • Outlook.com / Outlook on the web: open the email → ⋯ (More actions) → Download. The Outlook desktop app saves .msg files, which are not supported; use the web version.
    • Apple Mail (Mac): drag the email from the message list to the desktop, or choose File → Save As → Raw Message Source.
    • QQ Mail, NetEase 163/126 and other webmail: open the email and look for Export or Download email (.eml) in its More menu.
    • Mobile mail apps usually cannot export .eml; use the web version on a computer.
    • Download the email you received, not a forwarded copy: forwarding replaces the original headers with yours.
    • Temporary-inbox websites usually show only the rendered message, without the original headers.
    • An .eml contains your address and the full message. Upload only mail you are willing to have analyzed; files are limited to 2 MiB.

    Choose Gmail or Outlook.com only for an original message you downloaded from that service (Gmail: ⋮ → Download message; Outlook on the web: ⋯ → Download). The result then trusts that service’s own SPF/DKIM/DMARC check, and a verified official sender is not raised above low risk by wording alone. Choosing a service for mail it did not deliver can make a forged check look trusted.

    Optional, for one PNG/JPEG/WebP image. Original image bytes are not saved with a case. Browser OCR and QR results remain available for comparison.

    Additional readings can be wrong. They do not change the risk verdict; verify website addresses against the original image.

    Choose the language visible in the image. English avoids unrelated Chinese characters in English screenshots. Use mixed mode for bilingual images; always check extracted text.

    About This Analysis

    This analysis combines RFC 5322 headers, sender alignment, HTML links, attachments, and explainable content signals. When a validated text model is configured, its group-isolated model score and learned threshold are added without allowing weak text evidence to erase strong structural risk.

    If you already clicked, replied or entered something

    • Only opened the link, entered nothing: close the page, and check whether the browser downloaded a file; do not open it.
    • Entered a password: change it right away on the official website or app (not through this email), change it on every other site that uses the same password, and turn on two-step verification.
    • Entered a verification code or approved a sign-in request: this is more serious. Change the password, sign out of all other devices in the account’s security settings, and review recent sign-ins. For a bank or payment account, call the number on your card or in the official app to freeze it.
    • Opened an attachment, ran a program or installed remote-control software: disconnect from the internet, uninstall the remote-control software and run a full security scan. Changing passwords alone is not enough; change important passwords from another, clean device.
    • Sent money or gift cards: contact your bank or payment provider immediately, then report it to the police (in the US, also reportfraud.ftc.gov).

      Pipeline

      How It Works

      A layered detection pipeline designed to preserve strong phishing evidence.

      01
      Input Parsing
      Accept a sender address, subject/body text, or a complete RFC 5322 message. Raw messages expose headers, MIME parts, HTML, and attachment names.
      02
      Sender Identity Checks
      Evaluate suspicious domains, look-alike brands, risky TLDs, disposable patterns, and From / Reply-To / Return-Path alignment.
      03
      Message Structure Checks
      Inspect SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, dangerous attachments, IP and shortened URLs, and visible-link versus destination mismatches.
      04
      Explainable Content Rules
      Score urgency, credential requests, threats, obfuscation, and other suspicious behaviors. Attacker-controlled footer text cannot lower the score.
      05
      Optional Text Model
      Fit TF-IDF inside group-isolated cross-validation, keep campaign/template families together, and learn an F2-optimized phishing threshold from training folds.
      06
      Conservative Evidence Fusion
      Use the strongest credible evidence so a weak model score cannot average away clear structural or authentication risk.
